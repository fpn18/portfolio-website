Axi

Innovation and Entrepreneurship Project

Overview

Axi is a discreet haptic navigation wearable that allows users to keep their phone securely stored away, while receiving clear directional guidance. The device uses distinct vibration patterns for left turns, right turns, off-route alerts, and destination arrival. The companion app enables the user to set their route beforehand, after which navigation runs entirely in the background using MapBox API functionality.

  • Type: University Project
  • Role: UX/UI Designer, Marketing Strategist
  • Date: Oct 2025 – Dec 2025

Problem

Phone theft in London has escalated sharply in the last few years, with 62% of Londoners feeling more cautious using their phones outdoors and 70% of phone thefts being rapid snatch thefts targeting distracted individuals[1][2]. We conducted primary research to reinforce this problem; with students and older residents reporting consistently feeling unsafe using their phones in public, often keeping them hidden or tightly held. Our survey data also indicates high concern around theft, frequent distraction while navigating, and strong dependence on mobile navigation apps.

Current navigation methods exacerbate these risks - users must repeatedly check their screens, creating distraction while exposing their phones in high-theft areas. Existing hands-free options are limited and not designed for young urban travellers, being expensive and obtrusive. Therefore, the core problem is the lack of a simple, hands-free way to navigate cities without putting the user’s phone at risk and decreasing their spatial awareness.

Design Process

  • 1. Ideation & Concept Development

    Sketch exploration of multiple wearables (neck, wrist, hand, ring) allowed evaluation of ergonomics. The ring format was selected for discrete design, comfort, and effective vibration transmission, based on user feedback and feasibility. One of the main USPs for this product is that it blends into everyday use, a simple ring which can be fashionable as well as functional. Haptic patterns, app integration and practical specifications were then defined.

  • 2. Validation & Iteration
    • Expert validation highlighted issues such as reassurance and latency - feeding into refinements of the haptic pattern structure and light integration.
    • User testing demonstrated high recognisability of haptic signals and strong preference for finger placement.
    • Additional validations informed product feasibility, target segment optimisation, and pricing considerations.
    • 3. Value Creation & Business Integration

      The product’s design was continually aligned with user needs, resulting in a value proposition centred on safety, minimal distraction, efficient routing, and inclusive usability. Its business model, subscription strategy, and planned partnerships emerged from insights gathered during the design stages. Overall, the design process strategically combined research insights, iterative prototyping, user validation and commercial planning to create a solution that meaningfully improves safety and navigation experience in urban environments. One which instills confidence to explore and navigate without the need of a screen.

Our Business

Axi operates a subscription-led business model in which the wearable is provided with no upfront cost but requires the hardware to be returned after the subscription is ended. Revenue is generated through recurring subscriptions, B2B2C partnerships, and optional accessories. This structure reduces adoption barriers and prioritises long-term customer value - fits well with our target market who are on a lower budget.

Customer acquisition is driven through a mix of TfL tube advertising, social media ads and university and tourism partnerships, selected to reach young urban users. Funnel modelling shows a low weighted customer acquisition cost of £5.19, supported by high daily exposure and low-cost partner distribution.

Financial modelling demonstrates a strong customer lifetime value relative to acquisition cost, enabling profitability as the user base scales. While Year 1 reflects planned losses due to upfront marketing and operational investment, Axi reaches profitability in Year 2, with margins strengthening by Year 3 to £177,388, as recurring revenue grows and fixed costs stabilise.
